Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. In IB Biology HL you are expected to explain both the light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membranes and the light-independent Calvin cycle in the stroma, and to interpret experimental data on limiting factors. N1 flow check 2026-08-19.

Examiner note: candidates lose marks by describing the Calvin cycle as "dark reactions". Use light-independent.

Light-dependent reactions

Photosystem II absorbs a photon at 680 nm, exciting an electron that passes along the electron transport chain to plastoquinone, the cytochrome complex, and plastocyanin before reaching photosystem I. Photolysis of water replaces the lost electron and releases oxygen as a by-product.

Photolysis of water in the thylakoid lumen

Chemiosmosis through ATP synthase

Reduction of NADP+ to NADPH

Light-independent reactions

Ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase fixes carbon dioxide onto RuBP, producing two molecules of glycerate-3-phosphate. ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent stage reduce these to triose phosphate, of which five sixths regenerate RuBP.

No single limiting factor explains a field measurement; always state the factor you controlled.

Practical: limiting factors

Using pondweed in a sodium bicarbonate solution, count oxygen bubbles per minute at 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm and 40 cm from the lamp. Repeat three times and plot mean rate against light intensity, remembering the inverse square relationship.
