# The landing page: what Spectra is, and how the page tells it

A plan to argue with before anything is built. Part 1 decides what Spectra is for and who the page must win. Part 2
is the page, section by section, with its words. Part 3 is how it gets built so it never drifts from the app.

---

## Part 1. What Spectra is for

### Where it started, and where it is now

Spectra began as "the right AI for the right task": a router that picks a model. That is a feature, and a fading one,
because every assistant will route models soon. It is not why anyone would move their school life into a new app.

Look at what has actually been built since: the Library, a dozen kinds of work, Bridge, ManageBac sync, the island,
the overnight brief, live collaboration. They have one thing in common. A student's work is in pieces, scattered across
a school system, a cloud drive, a Downloads folder, a chat group, a chatbot and their own head. Every one of those
features exists to pull the pieces into one place and keep them there. The AI is good *because* it lives in that place:
it has read the notes, it knows the deadlines, it can open the file.

So, the purpose, in one internal sentence:

> **Spectra is the one place for a student's or a teacher's schoolwork: what they are set, what they make and what
> they know, on the web and on their own computer, with an intelligence that knows all of it and keeps it moving.**

### What the page must make people feel, in order

1. **"That's my life."** The scatter. Twelve apps that don't know about each other, and you as the glue between them.
2. **"Oh. It's all in one place."** Every kind of work, every deadline, every file, together and calm.
3. **"And it's alive."** Things move without you: deadlines arrive, plans make themselves, the morning is ready.
4. **"And it's still mine."** Files stay files on my computer. My teacher's date stays my teacher's. Nothing is locked
   in a cloud I can't leave.
5. **"I want this tomorrow."** For a student, show a teacher. For a teacher, show the head. For the head, a quiet
   page of answers and a door.

The order matters. Pain first, briefly and with humour, then the resolution, and never a list of features. Every claim
on the page is something the visitor watches happen on the real interface.

### The words

**Category.** Spectra is *the school workspace*. Not an AI tutor, not an LMS, not a notes app. Saying "workspace"
positions it next to the tools adults already respect (Notion, Figma) while "school" keeps it from sounding corporate.

**The brand line.** Recommended: **"All your schoolwork, in one place."** It is plain on purpose. Every visitor
understands it in one read, it survives translation, and the product does the impressive part. Alternatives worth
keeping on the wall:

- "Everything you learn, in one place."
- "Where schoolwork comes together."
- "One place that knows your work."

**The line for schools.** "ManageBac is where work is set. Spectra is where it gets done." It tells a coordinator, in
twelve words, that Spectra complements the system they already pay for instead of replacing it.

**The line about the AI.** "It has already read your notes." The visitor should feel the difference from pasting
things into a chatbot without anyone naming a competitor.

**Words we use:** work, notes, classes, deadlines, files, your computer, your teacher, tonight, tomorrow morning,
together, keep, make.

**Words we never use:** unlock, supercharge, seamless, revolutionise, AI-powered, productivity, leverage, ecosystem,
"Meet …", "Say goodbye to …", "game-changer", "next-generation", "all-in-one platform". No feature grid, no
uppercase eyebrow labels, no 01/02/03 numbering, no fake testimonials, no invented statistics, no "trusted by" logos
until there are real ones.

### Who the page must win

| Who | Their role | What they need from the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Students (IB, 14 to 18) | Use it every day; the reason it spreads | To recognise their evening in the first screen, and to want the product for themselves |
| Teachers | Champions; they bring it to the head | To see it saves *them* time, that collaboration is real, and that it respects the deadlines they set |
| Heads, IB coordinators, IT | Decide and pay | ManageBac on day one, no data entry, control over what the AI may do, where student work lives, how to start |

**The beachhead is IB schools on ManageBac.** Spectra already syncs classes, tasks, grades, submissions, files and
CAS. Naming ManageBac early is not narrowing the market; it is a flare that exactly the right people will see. Other
school systems come later and the page will say so then.

**Students can't buy, so give them a door anyway:** "Show this to your teacher" opens a short, prefilled message
with a link to the page (a mail draft or a copied link: nothing sent by us). This is the loop that gets Spectra into
staff rooms.

## Part 2. The page

### The idea: it was made in Spectra

Your original idea was right, and it is the spine of the page: **the landing page is a whiteboard artifact being built
in Spectra, and the visitor doesn't know until the end.** What went wrong last time was the mechanics, not the idea:
content was tied to scroll position, so everything assembled and disassembled under the visitor's thumb, and the cursors
competed for attention.

The fix, as rules:

- **Content never depends on scroll position.** Each section has one *build*: when it is about 40% in view, one or two
  cursors make one thing, in under two seconds, and it stays made. Scroll past quickly and you see the finished state.
  Scroll back and it is still built.
- **Only two moments are scrubbed by scroll**, because they are camera moves, not content: the prism flying into the
  island, and the final zoom out. Scrubbing a camera feels like flying; scrubbing content feels broken.
- **At most two cursors on screen.** Each has a name, a colour and a reason to be there. Humans move like people (the
  product's own cursor motion: hesitation, overshoot); Spectra's cursor glides and wears the spectrum.
- **A small cast that comes back.** It is a story about people, not features:
  - **Maya**, DP1, the student whose evening this is (hue: blue);
  - **Ms Reyes**, her History teacher (hue: orange);
  - **Spectra**, the spectrum cursor and the prism in the island.
- **The page takes place over one night.** It opens on a Sunday at 21:40 and ends on Monday at 07:00. A small clock
  and sky (Home's own `day-sky`) sit in the corner and move forward as the story does. That is what makes it feel
  continuous and personal: you are spending an evening with someone, and the evening has an ending.
- **Hidden canvas clues.** Now and then something a little *too* editable shows: a selection handle left on a heading,
  a sticky note from Maya ("move this up?"), a resolved comment pin. Nobody will notice them consciously. At the
  reveal they all make sense.

### The flow at a glance

```
 21:40  Hero ............. nine places → one place; the big prism
        Handover ......... the prism flies down and becomes the island (scrubbed)
        The glue ......... the evening it replaces, in one honest screen
        Ask .............. the island answers from Maya's own notes
        Make ............. one set of notes becomes a quiz, cards and slides
        Your computer .... Bridge: the file on the desktop and the work are the same file
        School ........... ManageBac: a deadline arrives and plans itself
        Together ......... Ms Reyes comments, Spectra helps, live
 23:58  Night ............ the page goes dark; Spectra keeps working
 07:00  Morning .......... the brief is ready
        For your school .. a letter to the head
        The reveal ....... zoom out: this page was a whiteboard in Spectra (scrubbed)
        The door ......... the island asks the last question
```

### 0. Hero: "Sunday, 21:40"

**What you see.** Warm paper. In the centre, the large, high-fidelity 3D prism, turning slowly, throwing a faint
spectrum onto the page beneath it. Floating around it at different depths (told apart by scale and tone, never blur),
nine pieces of a student's evening, each drawn exactly as it really looks:

- a ManageBac deadline notification, "History: Source analysis, due Friday";
- a file icon, `History IA - final (2).docx`;
- a cloud drive folder called "School stuff";
- a chat message in "DP1 Bio": "does anyone have the notes from friday";
- a chatbot reply: "I don't have access to your files.";
- a PDF from Downloads, `ch7_respiration_scan.pdf`;
- a sticky note in handwriting: "ask Ms Reyes about sources";
- a calendar in another tab, three things on Thursday;
- a photo of a classroom whiteboard, slightly crooked.

**Words.** Large serif, left of centre:

> **Your schoolwork is in nine places.**
>
> Spectra puts your classes, deadlines, files and ideas in one place, on the web and on your own computer, with an AI
> that already knows all of it.
>
> [Bring Spectra to your school]   Sign in

**The build** (on its own, about a second after load, not on scroll). The prism breathes in. The nine pieces slide
toward it one after another, are taken in, and with each one the prism's inner light gets a little brighter, a little
more spectral. As the last one goes in, a cursor strikes "nine places" in the headline and types "one place":

> **Your schoolwork, in one place.**

That is the whole product in four seconds: the pain, the resolution, and something alive doing it. A visitor who
leaves now still knows what Spectra is.

**Reused.** The prism model and shader from `src/app/landing/prism3d`; the type, buttons and tokens of the app.

### Handover: the prism becomes the island (scrubbed)

As the visitor starts to scroll, the camera does one continuous move. The big prism shrinks and sinks toward the
bottom centre of the window. Halfway down it loosens into the field's dots (the life engine's dissolve), and the dots
condense again into the island's small prism, and the composer draws itself out sideways from it, as it does in the
app. The island then stays docked at the bottom for the rest of the page, exactly where it lives in Spectra.

The island is the page's narrator from here on. Its notices announce things, its prism lights up when Spectra is
working, and its run tabs quietly show the sections already "done" on the right, like work finished in the app. It
is also the visitor's navigation: pressing a finished tab scrolls back to that section.

**Reused.** The real `Island` in showcase mode, `PrismLoader`, and the life engine's dissolve and condense.

### 1. The glue: the evening it replaces

One screen, honest and a little funny. No cards: a single wide line of browser tabs across the page that keeps adding
tabs as you arrive, until the titles are too narrow to read. Under it, three short observations set like margin notes:

> **School runs on a dozen apps that have never heard of each other. So you become the glue.**
>
> You paste your notes into a chatbot, and it forgets them by the next question.
>
> You have `essay.docx`, `essay_final.docx` and `essay_FINAL_actual.docx`, and you're not sure which one you sent.
>
> The deadline is in one app, the brief in another, the notes in a third, and it's 21:40 on a Sunday.

**The build.** Spectra's cursor arrives, and the tab strip closes in on itself, tab by tab, into a single tab labelled
"Spectra". The line under it: "One tab. Everything in it."

### 2. Ask: "It has already read your notes"

**What you see.** The island opens upward, in place, as it does in the app. A thread with Maya.

**The build.** Maya types: "What do I actually have to do for History this week? @History IA draft". The `@` turns
into a reference chip as she picks it. Spectra answers in three short sentences, each with a citation chip: one to
the ManageBac task, one to a paragraph of her draft, one to Ms Reyes's feedback on the last essay. As each chip
appears, a thin line of light runs from it to the source it cites, out on the board beside the island, and that source
glows for a moment.

**Words.**

> **Ask the one place that knows.**
>
> Spectra reads what you read and remembers what you wrote, so you never paste anything into it again. Every answer
> shows where it came from.

**Reused.** `Island`, the composer and its `@` references, the thread view, citation chips (`[[cite:…]]` rendering).

### 3. Make: "Every kind of work, on the same calm page"

**What you see.** The Library shelf: works as folders, exercise books and pages, with their real previews, filed
under Maya's classes. Small and quiet, with one of them in front: "Cell respiration notes".

**The build.** Maya drags the notes onto the island's prism. The prism takes them in, turns once, and deals out three
new works in a fan, like cards: a quiz, a set of flashcards and a five-slide deck. The top one opens like a book (the
short page-turn the app uses), showing the first question.

**Words.**

> **Make anything. Keep it all together.**
>
> Documents, slides, sheets, whiteboards, quizzes, flashcards and tasks, each one a page in the same place, filed with
> its class. Turn tonight's notes into tomorrow's quiz in the time it takes to ask.

**Reused.** `WorkCard` with real `work-preview` content, the Library shelf, the quiz and flashcard study views in
read-only showcase mode, the drop-onto-prism interaction (`data-life-receive`).

### 4. Your computer: "Your files stay yours"

**What you see.** Two windows side by side, drawn faithfully: a desktop file window showing `Documents › School ›
History`, and Spectra's Library. In the corner of the screen, the Bridge tray icon (the prism).

**The build.** Maya drags the History folder from her desktop into Spectra. The files fly in and file themselves
under the History class. Then the clever part: she opens `History IA.docx` in Word on the left and types a sentence;
the same sentence appears in Spectra on the right. Spectra's cursor fixes a typo in Spectra; the file's "modified"
time ticks over on the desktop.

**Words.**

> **It works with the files you already have.**
>
> Spectra Bridge links your own folders, so the file on your desktop and the work in Spectra are the same file. Edit it
> in Word or in Spectra; it is never copied, never uploaded twice, never "final (2)". Set up in a minute, from your
> first day.

**Reused.** The Devices pairing row and device card, the Library list, the Bridge status mark that sits on every
artifact.

### 5. School: "Your deadlines arrive by themselves"

**What you see.** ManageBac's beige task list on the left, exactly as students know it (the one coloured panel the
app allows). On the right, Spectra's week strip from Home.

**The build.** Ms Reyes posts a task; it appears in the ManageBac list; a moment later the island shows a notice:
"New in History: Source analysis, due Friday." The task slides across into Friday on the week strip. Spectra then
plans it: two time blocks settle into Wednesday and Thursday afternoon. Last beat: an older task receives a grade, and
"7 / 8" with Ms Reyes's feedback lands on it.

**Words.**

> **Connected to ManageBac from day one.**
>
> Every class, deadline, grade and piece of feedback arrives in Spectra as soon as it is posted, next to the work you
> are doing for it. Your teacher sets the date. You choose when to do the work.

**Reused.** `today-week`, the calendar blocks, the school task notice, island notices.

### 6. Together: "Work on it together, in the same place"

**What you see.** Maya's History IA document, open in Spectra's editor, with the workbar and presence.

**The build.** Ms Reyes's orange cursor arrives and leaves a comment on a sentence: "Your second source needs a date."
Maya replies "@Spectra can you find it?" Spectra's cursor glides to the source in the margin, adds the date and
resolves the comment. Three people, one page, no files sent back and forth.

**Words.**

> **Share any work, with anyone in your school.**
>
> Classmates and teachers see each other's cursors, comments land on the exact words they are about, and Spectra joins
> in when someone asks.

**Reused.** The document editor in showcase mode, comments and comment marks, presence cursors, the collaboration
interaction model the current landing already drives.

### 7. Night: "When you close the laptop"

**The turn.** As this section scrolls in, the clock in the corner runs from 23:58 forward and the whole page moves
into Spectra's dark theme: the same tokens, the same paper, at night. (This is the one place where scroll position
drives colour, because it is the passage of time.) The field's dots become a faint sky.

**What you see.** Nothing but the island, alone at the bottom, its prism working with a slow spectrum light. Above it,
in small type, the things it does while Maya sleeps appear one at a time and stay:

> Read the two pages Ms Reyes added.
> Planned the source analysis for Wednesday at 16:00.
> Made 12 flashcards for Thursday's Biology test.
> Wrote your morning.

**Words.**

> **Spectra keeps going after you stop.**
>
> Overnight it reads what changed, plans what comes next and has your morning ready before you wake up.

### 8. Morning: 07:00

**The turn.** Dawn. The sky lightens, the sun rises out of the field (Home's own sun), and the page returns to light.

**What you see.** Home, as Maya sees it at 07:00: "Good morning, Maya." and the brief, with its one-press actions.

**The build.** The brief's actions deal in one by one: "Source analysis: an hour set aside at 16:00. Open task",
"Biology test on Thursday: 12 flashcards ready. Study", "Ms Reyes replied to your comment. Open".

**Words.**

> **Wake up to a plan, not a pile.**

**Reused.** `home-day`, `today-brief`, `day-sky`, the brief actions.

### 9. For your school: a letter

The quietest section, and deliberately different: no animation, one sheet of Spectra paper set like a letter,
addressed to the people who decide. This is where heads and coordinators slow down, so it reads as a person talking,
not a slide.

> **To whoever looks after learning at your school,**
>
> ManageBac is where work is set. Spectra is where it gets done.
>
> It connects to ManageBac on the first day, so nobody enters a class, a student or a deadline by hand. Students'
> files can stay on their own computers through Bridge. Your school decides what Spectra AI may do, and you can
> change it at any time from the admin page. Teachers get the same place their students have, with the same calm.
>
> We would rather show you than tell you. Ask for a walkthrough, and we will set it up with your school's own classes.
>
> *The Spectra team*

Beside it, small and real: the admin People page and the AI controls, in showcase mode, at rest.

**To confirm before writing.** Where data is stored and processed, whether student work is ever used to train models,
retention, and GDPR status. These need to be true and exact; they are the questions buyers ask first.

### 10. The reveal: "This page was made in Spectra" (scrubbed)

**The move.** After the letter, the camera pulls back, scrubbed by scroll. The page the visitor has just read
shrinks, and it turns out to be one long whiteboard. Around it, Spectra resolves: the rail on the left, the workbar at
the top with the title "Landing page" and the History class's colour, three presence avatars (Maya, Ms Reyes,
Spectra), the island at the bottom with a finished run on its right: "Built the landing page".

All the canvas clues now make sense, and a few more are visible at this zoom: arrows between sections, a sticky note
with rejected headlines ("Supercharge your learning", crossed out, with Maya's comment: "no"), the nine pieces from
the hero pinned in a corner as the brief everyone started from.

**Words.** Set on the canvas, in Maya's handwriting-like sticky note style, not as a headline:

> Everything you just scrolled through was made here, by people and Spectra, in one place.

### 11. The door

The island takes the last word, the way it would in the app. Its prism lights up and it asks a question card, the
real `question-card`, with three answers:

> **Want one of these for your school?**
>
> [Bring Spectra to your school]   [Show this to my teacher]   [Sign in]

"Bring Spectra to your school" goes to the walkthrough request. "Show this to my teacher" opens a prefilled mail draft
or copies a link. Below, the footer: brand line, legal, contact.

### Motion, in a few rules

- **One build per section**, triggered once at about 40% in view, under two seconds, then still. Nothing loops except
  the prism's breathing.
- **Two scrubbed camera moves** (handover, reveal) and **one scrubbed passage of time** (night and morning). Nothing
  else is tied to scroll.
- **Fast scrolling skips builds**: anything entering at speed appears finished.
- **Reduced motion**: every section renders its finished state, the handover is a cross-fade, the reveal is a still.
- **Mobile**: the same story stacked, one cursor at a time, the island docked as on phones in the app.
- **The rail** (logo, Sign in, Get started) stays at the top until the reveal, then retires because the app's own rail
  has arrived.

### Ideas I would add

- **The visitor's own name.** The island's composer in the hero says "Ask Spectra anything". If the visitor types into
  it, Spectra answers from a small scripted set ("What is Spectra?", "Does it work with ManageBac?", "Is it free for
  students?") and scrolls to the section that shows the answer. Optional, but it turns "watching" into "using".
- **A time-of-day greeting.** Visit at 08:00 and the hero clock says your time, and the story starts from now instead of
  21:40. Small, personal, and the page is never the same twice.
- **The rejected headlines** at the reveal are a statement of taste: we are not that kind of product.

## Part 3. Building it so it never drifts from the app

The landing should be the app, wearing a costume: the real components, fed with a story's data, so that a change to
the island or a work card shows up on the landing the same day without anyone touching it.

**1. A showcase data source.** A `ShowcaseProvider` in `src/features/spectra/showcase/` holds one world: Maya, her
classes, works, tasks, thread, brief, Ms Reyes. The app's data hooks read from a small data-source context: Convex in
the app, the showcase store on the landing. Components themselves don't change. The fixtures are typed with the app's
own types, so a schema change breaks the build, not the page.

**2. Stages.** Each section renders real components inside a `ShowcaseStage`: it scales them to fit, turns off the
network, and routes clicks either nowhere or into the script.

**3. Cursors that find their targets by meaning.** Builds are written as beats against semantic targets (the
`data-*` targets the collaboration system already uses for real collaborators and the AI), never coordinates. If a
button moves in the app, the cursor follows it on the landing. The engine for this exists in the current landing
(`story/beats.ts`, `actors.ts`, the interaction model) and is kept; what changes is the trigger, from scroll scrub to
"play once, then hold".

**4. A contract test.** A test renders every stage with its fixtures and checks that every beat's target exists. If a
redesign removes something the landing points at, CI says so before visitors do.

**5. What is kept and what goes.** Keep the 3D prism (`prism3d`) for the hero, the story clock for the two camera
moves, and the beat and actor model. Retire the scroll-scrubbed scenes (`scenes/*`), which become set pieces built on
the real components.

**6. Performance.** One WebGL context, for the hero, released after the handover. Stages mount as they come near and
freeze when they leave. The night section reuses the app's dark tokens instead of a second stylesheet.

## Decisions for you

1. **The hero line.** The counted version ("in nine places" → "in one place") or the plain line from the start ("All
   your schoolwork, in one place.").
2. **The cast.** Maya and Ms Reyes, or other names; and whether the school is named (a fictional one).
3. **The humour at the reveal** (crossed-out "Supercharge your learning"): keep or cut.
4. **Price on the page**, or "ask for a walkthrough" only. For schools at this stage, a walkthrough converts better and
   keeps price flexible.
5. **The privacy facts** for the letter, which only you can confirm.
