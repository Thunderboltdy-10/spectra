Cellular Respiration — Overview

Key idea: Cells convert glucose into ATP through a series of enzyme-controlled reactions. This process powers nearly all life functions.

Cellular respiration occurs in three main stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

1. Glycolysis

  • Occurs in the cytoplasm.
  • Glucose (6C) is broken down into two molecules of pyruvate (3C).
  • Net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH.
Detailed steps (click to expand)

Glucose is phosphorylated, split into two three-carbon compounds, and oxidized to pyruvate.

2. Krebs Cycle

  • Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
  • Pyruvate is converted to acetyl-CoA and enters the cycle.
  • Produces NADH, FADH₂, and a small amount of ATP.

Remember: The Krebs cycle does not require oxygen directly, but depends on it indirectly to keep NAD⁺ and FAD available.

Krebs Cycle Mnemonic

Use the phrase “Keep Calm And Make Energy” to remember the main outputs.

3. Oxidative Phosphorylation

Electrons from NADH and FADH₂ pass through the electron transport chain, creating a proton gradient that drives ATP synthase.
