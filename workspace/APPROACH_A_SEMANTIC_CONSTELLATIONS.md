# Approach A: Semantic Constellations

## Positioning

This is the most spatial and most relationship-driven option.

It is not the recommended production-first shell, but it is valuable because it leans hardest into:

- cross-workstream memory,
- subject relationships,
- ambient AI connections,
- a feeling that the student's academic life is one map rather than many tabs.

## Core Metaphor

Not planets. Not rings. Not orbiting cards.

The better metaphor is:

- constellations,
- star fields,
- clusters of meaning,
- luminous landmarks in a shared academic sky.

Each workstream is an organic cluster, not a box inside a box.

## Why the earlier version was not enough

The previous orbit idea had two problems:

- rigid radial structures waste space and force awkward content layouts,
- the metaphor becomes more important than the work.

This version keeps the atmospheric readability without forcing circular behavior.

## Spatial Structure

```text
                  [Math cluster]
                     *   *
                  *    MATH    *
                       *   *

        [Physics cluster]     faint connection      [Engineering cluster]
            *  *   *  * ------------------------------ *   *  *   *
             * PHYSICS *                                * DESIGN *
            *  *   *  *                                *   *  *   *


                        [History cluster]
                          *  HISTORY  *
                        *    *   *    *
```

## What a cluster contains

A workstream cluster is composed of:

- one anchor title,
- category lanes that appear as soft spatial bands,
- content-shaped widgets positioned around that anchor,
- AI signals shown as glows, link hints, and suggested attachments.

Category lanes should feel like fields, not containers.

Example:

```text
        THREADS FIELD
     thread snippet     thread snippet

              WORKSTREAM TITLE

   DOCUMENTS FIELD         VISUALS FIELD
    note preview           quiz widget
    summary strip          concept map
```

## Distinct Visual Language

This shell should look the least gridded.

- asymmetrical cluster placement,
- content with soft auras rather than boxed cards,
- merged background glows when related items sit close together,
- radial or bloom-like local action menus,
- relationship hints that feel embedded in the space rather than overlaid.

Avoid making this shell look like normal cards that just happen to be farther apart.

## Hub Behavior

This shell should use the lightest hub presence.

- edges are nearly invisible until hover,
- the active timer can stay visible,
- the command surface can stay as a centered floating spotlight,
- reveal-all mode exposes utility items for rearrangement.

## Focus Mode

Entering a workstream should feel like descending into a constellation.

Sequence:

1. click cluster anchor or shortcut,
2. camera glides and enlarges the local field,
3. surrounding clusters dim but remain faintly legible,
4. local category fields strengthen,
5. widgets gain direct affordances.

### Motion Character

- lower-tension springs,
- slightly drifting arrivals,
- relationship lines that fade or breathe gently,
- soft rather than snappy transitions.

## Panel Choreography

Threads and documents still use the right push-panel.

Visual handoff:

- selected widget emits a light-trace toward the right,
- panel arrives as if it has condensed from the edge haze,
- command surface transfers into the panel footer.

## AI Behavior

This shell is strongest when AI is used to reveal connections.

Examples:

- "This chemistry summary relates to your biology enzymes workstream."
- "You have two visualizations that belong closer together."
- "I found overlap between calculus derivatives and motion graphs."

AI-generated objects should appear as new luminous nodes with provenance chips.

## Failure Modes

- too abstract for first-time users,
- too atmospheric and not productive enough,
- text overlap if density is not controlled,
- can become beautiful but vague.

## What Works

- strongest sense of one connected academic world,
- best shell for visualizing relationships and AI-discovered links,
- most memorable overview state,
- best option for students who think spatially across subjects.

## What Does Not Work

- weakest shell for quick operational productivity,
- easiest to over-design and under-clarify,
- hardest to keep from becoming "pretty but slow",
- most likely to hide the obvious thread/document actions behind atmosphere.

## Verdict

Keep it as a real preview shell, not the default.

It should influence the final product's overview and relationship language, but it should not dictate the main interaction model unless the team discovers that cross-subject synthesis matters more than direct day-to-day task throughput.

## Where it shines

- students managing interconnected subjects,
- concept-heavy revision,
- cross-disciplinary synthesis,
- higher-level overview moments.

## Preview Criteria

This shell succeeds only if:

- it never feels like stars for the sake of stars,
- focused work is still clear,
- widgets remain easy to read,
- the right panel remains grounded and practical.
